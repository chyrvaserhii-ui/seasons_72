72 Seasons 七十二候

The Japanese have watched the year in seventy-two pieces for a thousand years — one every five days. "East wind melts the ice". "Bush warblers start singing". "First frost falls". Seventy-two poetic names for the small shifts in nature.

What's inside

72 bundled illustrations

Ukiyo-e style, generated with Flux.1 from original prompts. No network needed at runtime.

72 classical haiku

Paired works by Bashō, Issa, Buson, Shiki, and Chiyo-ni — with Ukrainian and English translations.

Seasonal palette

Pink for spring, green for summer, orange for autumn, blue for winter. The whole app tints with the current season.

Home + Lock Screen widgets

Three iOS sizes plus an accessoryRectangular Lock Screen widget. The Large widget shows the previous, current, and next kō with their original engravings.

Moon phase

The traditional Japanese calendar was lunisolar. A small moon glyph on the Home tab shows the current phase — tap for next full and new moon dates.

Sekki tooltips

Tap any sekki name to read what the 24-season term traditionally meant — Kokuu grain rains, Seimei pure and clear, and 22 more.

Share cards

Tap share on any kō to export a 1080×1350 PNG with the illustration, name, and haiku — ready to post.

Quiet notifications

A gentle reminder at 9 AM on the first day of every new kō. Opt in from Settings.

Bilingual, offline

Ukrainian and English, system language by default. No accounts, no ads, no network calls.

Open source

Full source, all 72 data entries, and the image-generation scripts in one repo.

Where it comes from

The system of 72 micro-seasons reached Japan from China in the 6th century. In 1685, the shogun's astronomer Shibukawa Shunkai rewrote all 72 names based on observation of Japanese nature. His version — Honchō Shichijūni-kō (本朝七十二候) — is the one Japanese readers still use today. It's the one in this app.
